# Dating a Consultant

![Business consulting and professional relationships](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1560472354-b33ff0c44a43?ixlib=rb-4.0.3&ixid=M3wxMjA3fDB8MHxwaG90by1wYWdlfHx8fGVufDB8fHx8fA%3D%3D&auto=format&fit=crop&w=2126&q=80)

Hiring a consultant is a lot like dating in those TV sitcoms. You're dating this guy and it's promising but he just won't commit. When you're dating a consultant you probably should keep your options open. Just in case he really isn't as into you as you are into him.

## The Consultant Relationship Dynamic

Just like in romantic comedies, consultant relationships have predictable patterns:

### The Honeymoon Phase
- Everything seems perfect
- They promise to solve all your problems  
- You're excited about the possibilities
- They seem to understand you completely

### The Reality Check
- Projects take longer than expected
- Costs start to add up
- Their availability becomes questionable
- You wonder if they're seeing other clients

### The Commitment Issues
- They won't give you definitive timelines
- Everything is "it depends"
- They hedge their bets on deliverables
- Long-term planning feels impossible

## Managing Consultant Relationships

Here's how to protect yourself:

### Set Clear Expectations
- Define deliverables upfront
- Establish firm deadlines
- Agree on communication schedules
- Document everything

### Keep Your Options Open
- Don't put all your eggs in one consultant basket
- Maintain relationships with multiple vendors
- Have backup plans for critical work
- Build internal capabilities when possible

### Don't Fall Too Hard
- Remember it's a business relationship
- Evaluate performance objectively
- Be willing to end things if they're not working
- Don't let emotional attachment cloud judgment

## The Truth About Consultants

Most consultants are good people trying to do good work. But their business model creates natural tensions:

- They need to manage multiple clients
- Their expertise is their only asset
- They have to balance current work with future sales
- They often promise more than they can deliver

## The Bottom Line

In the end, just remember: **you don't have to marry the guy**.

Consultant relationships should be mutually beneficial, but they're ultimately temporary. Treat them as such, set appropriate boundaries, and don't be afraid to move on when the relationship isn't serving your needs.

The best consultant relationships are the ones where both parties understand this dynamic and work within it professionally.
